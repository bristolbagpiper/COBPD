About the band

Rooted in Bristol, built on discipline, camaraderie and live performance.

The band grew from local piping sessions in Hotwells into the City of Bristol's adopted pipe band. Today it brings together players from across the South West under Pipe Major Julian Smart, with a regular performing core and a growing learner group.

Where it started

From the back room of the Bear in Hotwells to major city events.

What began as a local gathering of pipers developed into a longstanding Bristol ensemble, now seen at remembrance parades, civic occasions, festivals and private celebrations throughout the region.

How it works

A volunteer band with charitable aims and a serious approach to playing.

Members are unpaid for performances. Income from bookings goes straight back into the practical work that keeps the band moving: instruments, uniforms, hall hire, repairs and travel.

Join the band

Experienced pipers and drummers are welcome, and beginners can grow into the line-up.

Whether you're already playing or starting from scratch, the band can help point you toward the right next step. Learners need commitment, regular practice and the ability to travel to weekly rehearsals, but support is available from tutors and established players.

  • Weekly group rehearsals in central Bristol
  • Guidance for pipers, snare, tenor and bass drummers
  • A clear route from tuition to the performing set
  • A friendly but disciplined rehearsal culture

FAQ

Common questions, answered clearly.

Can you play at an event if we've seen you somewhere else first?

Yes. The band is happy to discuss suitable events, and the best first step is to use the booking page or email so the enquiry reaches the right person quickly.

How much does a booking cost?

Pricing depends on the event, location, duration and whether you need the full band, a smaller ensemble or an individual performer.

Where and how often do you rehearse?

The band rehearses weekly in central Bristol.

Can beginners join even if they have never played before?

Yes, but it takes steady commitment. Learners need to practise independently as well as with the band, and tuition support may be recommended on the path to becoming performance-ready.

I already play drums. Can I come straight in on snare?

Sometimes, but pipe band snare has its own technique and repertoire. New players may need focused tuition and may progress through other roles before stepping into the regular snare line.
